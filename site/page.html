Scoring logic

Four pillars, sixteen signals

I score every fund on the same formula, so the ranking can be checked and argued with. This tab walks through it in four steps, shows what happens when a brand has more than one signal, and lets you change every weight.

Step 1 · Scale

Each signal becomes 0 to 1

best fund = 1 · worst = 0

Counts such as brands held are log-scaled first, so one 16-brand portfolio doesn't flatten everyone else.

Step 2 · Pillars

Signals add up into four pillars

pillar = Σ weight × signal

Each pillar is a weighted sum of its signals; the weights inside a pillar add up to 100%.

Step 3 · Score

Pillars add up into one score

Access and strategic value are scaled down for funds with fewer than three live brands: reach is worth little without brands to reach.

Step 4 · Tier

The score sets the tier

A ≥ 38 and 3+ brands · B ≥ 30

The Ranking tab shows each fund on the tier scale and why it landed where it did.

When a brand shows more than one signal

Brand pain is where most of the signals live. Two rules decide how several signals combine: older news counts for less, and signals that land close together count for more, because that overlap is the moment a brand is most likely to buy.

Rule 1 · Recency

News loses half its weight every 9 months

A story from this month counts in full. One from 18 months ago counts a quarter. Anything older than 24 months isn't counted. Open job ads are live today, so they don't decay.

Rule 2 · Stacking

Signals in the same 12 months multiply

I count four kinds of trigger: a new listing, a funding round, ops hiring and export news. Each extra trigger inside 12 months adds 25% to the brand's pain, up to 1.5×, so one very busy brand can't dominate its fund.

How tiers work

AScore of 38 or more and at least 3 live brands. Call these first: one partner opens several doors.
BScore of 30 or more, or a high score with fewer than 3 live brands. Worth an intro, but it won't compound.
CScore below 30. A reserve bench: few brands, thin signals, or both.

Read with care

What the data can and can't tell you

Coverage

Known gaps

Most small UK companies file abbreviated accounts without turnover, so size comes from the accounts type filed at Companies House (micro, small, medium, full or group), with retail reach as the fallback. A missing retailer means no product page, Open Food Facts entry or stockist list confirmed one; manufacturers and own-label makers rightly have none. Job boards add little: most of these brands hire through LinkedIn or recruiters. Scores run low because these signals are sparse: read them as a ranking, not a grade.

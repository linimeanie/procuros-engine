Cold doesn't work here. Borrow the introduction.

Procuros is unknown in UK grocery, and a brand's buying moment (a retailer mandate, a raise, a new listing, an ERP move) is invisible from outside. When it arrives they ask someone they already trust. Their investors are that someone, they can see the moment coming, and an introduction from them costs Procuros nothing but a good reason to make it.

Opportunities

Rank the opportunity, not just the fund

An opportunity is one fund introducing Procuros to one brand. It's only worth pursuing when the brand needs EDI and the fund can actually make the intro, so its score multiplies the two.

The unit is a door, not a fund

A fund is not an opportunity. A fund plus a particular brand is. So every fund–brand pair was assessed on its own, and the ranking of funds is simply what their strongest pairs add up to. That one choice decides everything else below.

Brand fit: what an ICP brand looks like

Procuros automates order, delivery and invoice documents between suppliers and retailers. The brands that need it sell through several grocers and are changing fast. Each part below is marked as confirmed (a source shows it), assumed (inferred from something else) or not observable.

PartWhat it measuresEvidenceWeight

No double counting: retail reach is where a brand sells today, why-now is what changed in the last 24 months. Volume of news is deliberately not a signal, because it would count the same events a second time.

Why now: two rules for recent events

New listings, funding, ops hiring and export news each mark a moment when order volumes and retailer connections grow. Older news counts for less, and events that land close together count for more. Funding is read from Companies House filings (share allotments and new charges) as well as the press, because a filing is dated, authoritative and often lands months before anyone writes it up. Every item below names the source it came from, so a filing and a blog post can be told apart. Consumer reviews and taste tests are excluded: they are not events, however many funding words their headlines contain.

Rule 1 · Recency

News loses half its weight every 9 months

A story from this month counts in full, one from 18 months ago counts a quarter, and anything older than 24 months isn't counted. Open job ads are live, so they don't decay.

Rule 2 · Stacking

Events in the same 12 months multiply

Each extra kind of event in the same 12 months adds 25% to the brand's why-now score, capped at 1.5×.

Intro path: can the fund open the door?

Points for each piece of evidence that the fund can reach the brand's decision-makers. Two of these are entry conditions rather than evidence: a pair only exists because the fund lists the brand, and a sold holding is set aside entirely. So no pair ever scores zero here, and a score alone never means there is someone to ask.

From pairs to a fund ranking

Pair score

pair = 100 × brand fit × intro path

Multiplying rather than adding means a weak side drags the whole pair down instead of being averaged away. It does not sink it: both halves carry a floor, which is why a score alone is not enough to call a pair worth pursuing.

Fund score

Best pair counts fully, the second half, the third a quarter. Depth helps, but only on top of quality: four strong pairs beat fifteen weak ones.

Groups

Call first needs two pairs worth pursuing (+ and a named person on each), so one relationship opens more than one door. The group lines use score alone, which is why three funds in Next still have nobody to ask.

Does the weighting change the answer?

The ranking was rerun under other reasonable weightings. Asking whether the same funds stay in the 20 would prove nothing, because only funds hold a fitting brand at all. What matters is whether the order holds, so each row reports the largest rank move that weighting causes. The call-first group is stable; the middle of the table is not.

Nothing above observes the thing that actually matters.

Whether a brand keys its orders in by hand is not public. No filing records it, no trade story reports it, and no stockist page implies it. Every signal on this page (how many grocers, how recently something changed, what size the accounts suggest) is a proxy for a fact none of them can see.

That is not a hole to apologise for, it is the reason the first call is fifteen minutes and starts with how do orders from each grocer arrive today. The model's job is to decide whose fifteen minutes are worth asking for. It cannot do the asking.

What to trust

Where this is solid, and where it isn't

The ranking is only worth as much as the evidence under it, and that evidence is uneven. This says which parts are sourced, which are inferred, which are my judgement, and which could not be seen at all. The last group is the one to ask about on the first call.

What counts as a door

A brand counts only if the fund still holds it and it isn't already a Procuros customer. There is no revenue ceiling, because none is needed. Of the seven brands I could identify as clearly above the ICP band, six had already been exited by the fund holding them, so the holding test removes those anyway. The seventh, Bright Blue Foods, is still held and scores 3 against a qualifying bar of 20. Applying a ceiling as well changed the ranking by nothing. Companies House publishes accounts type rather than turnover, so a ceiling cannot be derived the way the floor can, and a list of names applied by recognition would only ever catch the brands someone recognised. The useful version is the observation rather than the rule: investors do not still hold brands that large.

Judgement calls

These are my calls rather than scraped data:

  • The weights for brand fit and the points for intro path. The call-first group survives every weighting tested; the middle of the table does not, and the Method tab reports how far each scenario moves it.
  • Where the size bands sit: micro filers score low as below the band, and full or group accounts are treated as uncertain rather than large.
  • The 9-month half-life for news and the 1.5× stacking cap.
  • The group lines for Call first and Next.

Known gaps

Most small UK companies don't publish turnover, so size comes from the accounts type filed at Companies House, with retail reach as the stand-in. A board link needs the person's name on both the fund's team page and the brand's Companies House officer list, so a partner missing from the team page is missed. Job boards add little: these brands mostly hire through LinkedIn and recruiters.

    One motion, run twice.

    Task 1.1 says which doors to knock on. It does not say whether anyone opens them. Six weeks on the top two funds answers that, before the motion is worth taking to anyone else.

    The ask is identical at both funds, and the only thing that varies is the fund itself. Running a different ask at each would look like a test of how best to ask, but with one fund per arm and half a dozen other differences between them, no result could be attributed to the ask. A clean read on one variable is worth more than a comparison nobody can interpret.

    Why these two

    They are #1 and #2 on the shortlist and each has a fund person on a target brand's board, which is the condition the motion needs. One is PE and one is VC, which is an observation we have a hypothesis about rather than a variable we can test with one of each.

    What is held constant, and what cannot be

    Everything we control is identical at both funds: the same asset, the same wording, one named brand, one ask. What differs is only what the funds are. Keeping the ask fixed costs us a comparison we were never entitled to make, and buys a clean read on the one thing we can actually change next time.

    What we give first

    The obvious thing to send is a one-page summary of which grocers a brand sells through and which documents each one exchanges. A brand knows which grocers it sells to, so there is nothing in that they do not already have, and an investor forwarding it spends credibility rather than earning it.

    What a brand genuinely cannot produce is what document errors are costing them, because the evidence sits in remittance advices nobody reads that way. So the asset is a short brief on the regulator's own numbers plus a check the brand can run on its own data. Built once, sent to every fund and every brand, with three lines of personalisation.

    Part 1 · the numbers

    What the regulator found

    In the Groceries Code Adjudicator's 2026 annual survey of more than 2,600 suppliers, published 16 April 2026, the single most-reported problem in the sector was retailer processes for promptly resolving invoice discrepancies, raised by 20% of suppliers, ahead of inaccurate forecasting (18%), delisting without notice (15%) and late payment (14%). Its 2016 Tesco report found unilateral deductions unreasonable and required 30 days to challenge them. Sourced, dated, and not from a vendor.

    Part 2 · the mechanism

    How a deduction happens

    A despatch note that arrives after the lorry, an invoice whose line total disagrees with the order, a missing location code. Each one is a document that moved by hand. One page, no jargon, so a founder can follow it.

    Part 3 · the check

    Thirty minutes, their own data

    Take one period's remittance advices, reconcile them against invoices raised, and count two things: deductions with no explanation attached, and days to resolve the ones that had one. They have this data. Nobody has cut it this way. They come back with a number, which is a conversation rather than a read.

    Why an investor forwards this: it makes them useful to their founder, which is the only reason anyone hits forward. It cites a regulator rather than us, it asks the brand to look at their own numbers, and Procuros appears once, at the end. The same brief goes to every fund, so the cost of the next fund is an email, which is the part of this that has to survive Task 1.3.

    The personalisation, per brand

    Three lines on top of the shared brief, from evidence already collected in Task 1.1: where this brand sells, how many document streams that implies, and what changed recently. The envelope, not the contents.

    The premise of all of this is still untested.

    Task 1.1 argues that cold does not work and that an investor's introduction carries trust Procuros cannot buy. Nothing in these six weeks checks that. If Rory forwards the brief and Borough Broth takes the call, we still will not know whether Borough Broth would have answered a direct email, and that difference is the entire reason to run this motion rather than a sequencer.

    Measuring it needs a cold arm: fifteen matched brands, same asset, same ask, no investor. It is left out here on purpose, and the reason is scope rather than rigour. These six weeks are about whether an investor forwards anything at all, and running a cold cell at the same time would mean approaching fifteen brands we have not yet earned the right to approach. It is the first thing Task 1.3 has to establish, and it is named there as such rather than assumed away.

    Kill criteria

    When to stop, decided now so we don't argue about it later.

    Silence

    Nobody has replied by day 14

    Both deal leads written to twice, both backups used, nothing back from any of them. That is not a fund problem, it is the email. Rewrite the opening before adding a third fund, because sending the same thing to Active Partners would only buy the same silence more expensively.

    Stalled

    Replies, but zero forwards by day 28

    They answered and declined to pass it on. This is the most informative failure available and the reason the brief is written to be forwardable. Get the reason in their words, and treat it as the finding rather than a setback.

    Wrong brand fit

    The brand calls say they don't need us

    If orders already flow automatically at a brand with four or five grocers, the brand-fit scoring in Task 1.1 is measuring the wrong thing. Fix the model before scaling it to a hundred funds.

    Relationship

    Anyone asks us to stop

    If a fund or a founder says the introduction felt pushed, stop at once and apologise. One relationship in this market is worth more than one brand, and both of these funds back several.

    The read-out on 11 December

    The three numbers that decide Task 1.3

    Forward rate per ask, hours per introduction, and the distribution of reasons for a no. Those three say whether there is a channel here, what it costs, and where it breaks. Every touch is logged to produce them: date, fund, contact, channel, outcome, and the reason in their words.

    If nothing forwards

    Then the route is not the problem. The offer is, or the brand-fit model is. Either way we go back to the brand calls and the scoring before taking this to a third fund, let alone to all of them.

    Procuros · UK go-to-market · Task 1.3 · scaling the motion

    A good wedge. A poor engine.

    Assume Task 1.2 works: investors forward, brands take the call, some of them buy. The question is what happens when this is pointed at every VC and PE firm in the UK. The honest answer is that the motion has a ceiling, it is set at the top rather than at conversion, and it is probably lower than the 10% of new-business ARR this is meant to contribute.

    That is not an argument against running it. It is an argument for knowing what it is: a high-trust, low-volume channel that opens accounts nothing else opens, which needs something else beside it for volume.

    What the list is actually worth

    The customer is a brand, not a fund, so this counts brands. The rows above the line are measured from the Task 1.1 pipeline. Below it are the assumptions this motion is built on: that an introduction from a brand's own investor changes how the brand responds. Task 1.2 is what replaces them with measured rates.

That is stage 1, and its job is proof rather than volume. The volume comes next. The binding constraint is that 3,710 portfolio companies yield 23 strong asks. This channel is supply-limited, and the supply is brands that fit, not funds.

    From one door to the whole portfolio

    The table above counts the channel one brand at a time, which is only its first stage. The reason to go through investors at all is that one relationship can open a whole portfolio. That cannot be asked for on day one, so the motion runs in two stages: earn it with one introduction, then expand it to everything the fund holds.

    Stage 1 · earn

    One named brand, one ask

    Exactly Task 1.2: a researched introduction to one brand, a call, and a report back to the fund. Its job is not volume. It proves to the fund that we are useful to its founders and do not waste their time, which is the only thing that buys stage 2.

    Stage 2 · expand

    The fund opens its portfolio

    Once an introduction has gone well and been reported back, ask the portfolio or platform team for portfolio-wide access: a place on its perks or preferred-vendor list, a slot in its founder newsletter, or thirty minutes with its portfolio's ops and finance leads to run the remittance check together. One yes reaches every brand the fund holds, not one.

    The gate between them

    No stage 2 without a stage 1

    A fund is only asked to open its portfolio after a brand it introduced has taken the call and the fund has heard how it went. Asking for the whole portfolio first is a vendor pitch to an investor, which is the cold outreach this motion exists to avoid.

    This is where the volume is. Stage 1 sets the order and proves the channel; stage 2 is why the channel exists. The concentration helps: Active Partners alone holds eight target brands, all of which clear the bar, so one portfolio-wide yes there is worth eight single introductions. Widening to beauty and other consumer goods is the second lever, and Procuros already has beauty reference customers in Flaconi and Niche Beauty. Dan Stern, Piper's backup contact, already sits on Vieve's board.

    The pool is finite, and it does not refill.

    Cold outbound renews: the same brand can be approached again next quarter with a better reason. This cannot. A fund forwards an introduction once or twice a year before the goodwill thins, and once the UK universe has been worked through, new asks only appear when a fund makes a new investment or a portfolio brand lands a new grocer.

    So this is a stock to be spent carefully, not a pipeline to be run continuously. That single fact is what makes most of the principles below worth protecting: each one exists because the cost of getting it wrong is a door that does not reopen.

    Five things to protect

    Each of these is true at two funds and easy to lose at a hundred. They are ordered by what it costs to break them.

    1. 1Never approach a brand a fund declined to introduceThis is the one that ends the channel rather than one relationship, which is why it is first. The brief notes these brands are closely connected to each other; their investors are more so. Going around a no gets discussed, and it would be the single most expensive mistake available here.
    2. 2Never ask without a named person and a named brandThe moment an email opens "we help grocery brands" it is cold outreach wearing a warm coat, and an investor's inbox treats it accordingly. If the research has not produced a human and a dated trigger, there is no ask to make. Skip the fund rather than soften the ask.
    3. 3Report back on every introduction, including the failuresThe second ask is bought with the first report. At two funds this is courtesy; at a hundred it is the only thing that converts a one-off favour into a standing channel, and it is the first thing dropped when volume rises.
    4. 4Keep the give-first asset free, useful and the same for everyoneBuilt once, sent to all, no gate and no form. The temptation at scale is to thin it or to make it a lead magnet; either turns a forwardable document back into marketing, and the whole motion rests on an investor being willing to attach it.
    5. 5Say no out loud when it isn't a fit"If it isn't a fit I'll tell them so" has to be true, and visibly so, because the investor is lending their judgement and not just their address book. One wasted founder call costs more introductions than one won deal returns.

    Six failure modes, and the earliest signal of each

    Ordered by how soon they would bite. The point of naming the signal is that most of these are cheap to catch early and expensive to discover at fund fifty.

    What I'd change before fund three

    Price the warm premium first

    Everything else is secondary to knowing whether the investor is adding anything. Fifteen matched brands, same asset, same ask, no investor, run alongside the next two funds. If the warm reply rate is not clearly above cold, this motion is an expensive way to send email and the honest move is to say so and reallocate, which is a better outcome than scaling it quietly for two quarters. If it is clearly above, that number becomes the argument for funding the research step properly.

    Build the register

    One row per brand: who was approached, by which fund, when, and what came back. Dull, and the thing that breaks first without it.

    Gate on brands, not funds

    Only funds with two or more qualifying brands earn the full research treatment. Everything else is a watchlist until a portfolio changes.

    This should be one of two motions, not the plan.

    Around four customers from today's 23 strong asks, and around seventeen once the funds open their portfolios, is a good return on a channel that opens doors nothing else opens. Whether it reaches a tenth of new-business ARR depends on deal size and the new-business target, which this analysis does not have; on its own it is unlikely to. The brands this reaches are the ones worth having: well connected, visible, and quotable to the next prospect. So its real value may be reference and brand awareness rather than bookings, which is exactly what the ICP note in the brief argues.

    What belongs beside it is the retailer-mandate trigger: when a grocer tells its suppliers to go digital, every affected brand becomes a prospect on the same day, with a deadline attached and no need for an introduction. That is the volume motion. This is the trust motion. Running one without the other is what breaks the number.

    There is also a bigger pool behind the same relationships. This list is grocery only, because the brief's ICP is grocery. The same portfolios hold about 3,600 other companies, and Procuros already sells beyond grocery, into beauty (Flaconi and Niche Beauty are reference customers). Widening the ICP to beauty and other FMCG would multiply the asks without a single new investor relationship. Grocery still goes first, because it is where references travel fastest.

    Procuros · UK go-to-market · Task 2 · the non-scalable idea

    A dinner where Procuros doesn't speak.

    Fourteen people around one table in London. Two of them already use Procuros and spend twenty minutes taking their own order flow apart in front of the others: what broke, what it cost, what they tried first. The rest run grocery brands of roughly the same size and recognise every word of it. Nobody from Procuros presents anything.

    This does not scale, on purpose. One evening a quarter, a guest list built by hand, and a host who has to be persuaded rather than booked. That is the cost of the only thing in this market that outperforms a vendor saying it about itself.

    The brief already contains the argument for this.

    Procuros picked grocery brands partly because they are "very well connected amongst each other, so that penetrating this segment, if done well, is a lot easier than in other verticals." That is a statement about a dense referral graph. Cold email, paid acquisition and even the investor motion in Task 1 all route around that graph. A dinner routes through it.

    And the pain Procuros sells against is one nobody admits to in public. No founder posts that their team keys purchase orders in by hand, or that a grocer deducted four thousand pounds last quarter and they never found out why. They will say it across a table to someone with the same problem, which is the whole reason the room is worth building.

    Who is actually in the room

    The guest list is the product. Get it wrong and it is a networking dinner, which is worth nothing to anyone.

    Two hosts, deliberately mismatched

    Tony's Chocolonely is the draw: people accept an invitation with that name on it, and it is a Procuros customer selling through the UK grocers. TrueStart Coffee is the proof: a UK brand closer to the size of everyone else at the table, so the story sounds achievable rather than aspirational. One brand gets people in the room and the other makes them believe it applies to them. A single host does one of those jobs, not both.

    Four brands, invited in pairs

    The founder and whoever runs their orders or finance. Founders accept dinner invitations; ops leads answer the questions. Inviting in pairs puts the decision-maker and the actual user at the same table, which is the exact definition of "qualified" used in Task 1.2. Four rather than six because eight guests plus hosts, investors and us is already fourteen, and a table much past that stops being one conversation.

    One or two investors

    Starting with JamJar, which backs both hosts. Their portfolio peers are in the room and Procuros is visibly credible without having said anything.

    Procuros is outnumbered at least four to one and attends to pour wine and answer questions when asked. The moment the vendor is the most senior voice at the table, the format has failed and everyone can feel it.

    The evening

    No slides, no stand, no roundtable branding. A long table and one structured twenty minutes.

    1. 1The teardown, not the testimonialThe host walks through their real retailer document flow: which grocers, which documents, what used to arrive by email, who used to key it in. Specific and unflattering: the version with the mistakes left in. A testimonial is an advert with a customer's face on it; a teardown is a peer admitting something.
    2. 2The numberOne figure from their own books: hours a week recovered, or deductions that stopped. Said by them, not by us, which is the entire point of the format.
    3. 3What they tried firstThe portal, the spreadsheet, the part-time ops hire, the quote from a traditional EDI provider. Everyone at the table is somewhere in that sequence and will place themselves in it immediately.
    4. 4Questions, to the hostProcuros answers only what the host passes over. If no question reaches us, the evening still worked.
    5. 5One ask, at the endNot a meeting. The same thing the investor forwarded in Task 1.2: run the half-hour check on one quarter of your own remittances. It is free, it is their data, and it turns a pleasant evening into a number they bring back to us.

    What success looks like, decided beforehand

    Dinners are where go-to-market measurement goes to die, so the measures are set before the invitations go out.

    What we countTarget for one dinnerWhy this one

    Remittance checks run3 within 14 daysThe only ask made. It is the difference between a good night and a pipeline.

    Where this breaks

    The host sounds like an advert

    The single biggest risk, and it is caused by us. If the host is over-briefed, handed talking points, or told which number to say, the room detects it within a minute and the evening is worse than having sent nothing, because everyone present now associates Procuros with being handled. The mitigation is uncomfortable: agree the structure and let them say what they actually think, including the parts where Procuros was slow or the integration was harder than promised. A host who volunteers one genuine complaint buys more credibility than any case study.

    Free dinners have high no-show

    Ops leads cancel on the day far more than founders do, and they are the guests who matter. Over-invite by a third, confirm by phone rather than email, and accept that a table of ten with the right people beats fourteen with the wrong ones.

    It only works in London, and only quarterly

    A real ceiling, not a caveat. Four brands an evening and four evenings a year reaches sixteen brands, which is not a growth channel by any reading. It is a credibility engine whose output is the references and the nominations that make every other motion cheaper.

    It also fixes the weakest link in Task 1.

    The investor motion asks a partner to spend their own credibility introducing a vendor they have never seen work. That is the real reason a forward rate might be low, and no amount of rewriting the email solves it. An investor who has sat at this table, watched two of their peers' portfolio brands take their order flow apart, and heard a number said out loud, is a different proposition when the ask arrives.

    Which suggests the sequencing in the submission is backwards. The dinner should come first and Task 1.2 should follow it, because the cheapest way to raise a forward rate is not a better subject line. It is having been in the room.

Warm Doors UK

Procuros · UK go-to-market · Task 1.1 · data as of

Twenty warm doors into UK grocery.

UK grocery brands don't buy EDI from cold emails. They buy when a retailer mandates it, a funding round lands, or the ops team drowns in PDF orders. Their investors see those moments first. We scraped the portfolios of every UK fund that backs grocery brands, scored each brand for retail reach and live pain, and ranked the investors who can open the most doors.

Why go through investors

Trust

An intro beats a cold sequence

A portfolio partner recommending a vendor carries the weight of a board seat. Founders take that meeting.

Density

One relationship, many brands

A single fund can hold 5 to 16 UK grocery brands. Win the partner once and the intros compound.

Timing

Money changes the ops stack

New rounds and buyouts come with 100-day plans: new retailers, new ERP, new pressure on order processing. That is Procuros' window.

How the data was built

Stockist pages for named retailers, careers pages for ops and supply-chain roles, and 24 months of news for funding, listings and exports.

Scoring logic

Four pillars, sixteen signals

Each signal is scaled 0 to 1 across the funds; counts are log-scaled so one huge portfolio doesn't flatten the rest. Access and strategic value only count once a fund holds brands to reach: they scale with live brands up to three. Move the sliders to re-weight the pillars and watch the ranking change.

What the data can and can't tell you

Coverage

Known gaps

No revenue data reached the pipeline, so the £3m–£100m size band is approximated from retail reach and trade-press coverage. A missing retailer means the brand's own site doesn't name it, not that it isn't stocked there. Scores run low because these signals are sparse: read them as a ranking, not a grade.

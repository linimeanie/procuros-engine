Warm Doors UK

Procuros · UK go-to-market · Task 1.1 · data as of

Twenty warm doors into UK grocery.

UK grocery brands don't buy EDI from cold emails. They buy when a retailer mandates it, a funding round lands, or the ops team drowns in PDF orders. Their investors see those moments first. We scraped the portfolios of every UK fund that backs grocery brands, scored each brand for retail reach and live pain, and ranked the investors who can open the most doors.

Why go through investors

Trust

An intro beats a cold sequence

A portfolio partner recommending a vendor carries the weight of a board seat. Founders take that meeting.

Density

One relationship, many brands

A single fund can hold 5 to 16 UK grocery brands. Win the partner once and the intros compound.

Timing

Money changes the ops stack

New rounds and buyouts come with 100-day plans: new retailers, new ERP, new pressure on order processing. That is Procuros' window.

Scoring logic

Four pillars, sixteen signals

Each signal is scaled 0 to 1 across the funds; counts are log-scaled so one huge portfolio doesn't flatten the rest. News loses half its weight every 9 months, and a brand showing two or more triggers within 12 months (a listing, a round, ops hiring, export) gets up to 1.5× on its pain score, because that overlap is the buying moment. Access and strategic value only count once a fund holds brands to reach: they scale with live brands up to three. Move the sliders to re-weight the pillars and watch the ranking change.

Read with care

What the data can and can't tell you

Coverage

Known gaps

Most small UK companies file abbreviated accounts without turnover, so size comes from the accounts type filed at Companies House (micro, small, medium, full or group), with retail reach as the fallback. A missing retailer means no product page, Open Food Facts entry or stockist list confirmed one; manufacturers and own-label makers rightly have none. Job boards add little: most of these brands hire through LinkedIn or recruiters. Scores run low because these signals are sparse: read them as a ranking, not a grade.

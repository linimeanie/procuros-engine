Brand fit: what an ICP brand looks like

Procuros automates order, delivery and invoice documents between suppliers and retailers. The brands that need it sell through several grocers and are changing fast. Each part below is marked as confirmed (a source shows it), assumed (inferred from something else) or not observable.

PartWhat it measuresEvidenceWeight

No double counting: retail reach is where a brand sells today, why-now is what changed in the last 24 months. The old "volume of news" signal is gone because it counted the same stories twice.

Why now: two rules for recent events

New listings, funding, ops hiring and export news each mark a moment when order volumes and retailer connections grow. Older news counts for less, and events that land close together count for more. Funding is read from Companies House filings — share allotments and new charges — as well as the press, because a filing is dated, authoritative and often lands months before anyone writes it up. Every item below names the source it came from, so a filing and a blog post can be told apart. Consumer reviews and taste tests are excluded: they are not events, however many funding words their headlines contain.

Rule 1 · Recency

News loses half its weight every 9 months

A story from this month counts in full, one from 18 months ago counts a quarter, and anything older than 24 months isn't counted. Open job ads are live, so they don't decay.

Rule 2 · Stacking

Events in the same 12 months multiply

Each extra kind of event in the same 12 months adds 25% to the brand's why-now score, capped at 1.5×.

Intro path: can the fund open the door?

Points for each piece of evidence that the fund can reach the brand's decision-makers. Two of these are entry conditions rather than evidence: a pair only exists because the fund lists the brand, and a sold holding is set aside entirely. So no pair ever scores zero here, and a score alone never means there is someone to ask.

Pair score

pair = 100 × brand fit × intro path

Multiplying means either side at zero sinks the pair: a perfect brand with no route is worth as little as a great route to the wrong brand.

Fund score

Best pair counts fully, the second half, the third a quarter. Depth helps, but only on top of quality: four strong pairs beat fifteen weak ones.

Groups

Does the weighting change the answer?

The ranking was rerun under other reasonable weightings. Asking whether the same funds stay in the 20 would prove nothing, because only 22 funds hold a fitting brand at all. What matters is whether the order holds, so each row reports the largest rank move that weighting causes. The call-first group is stable; the middle of the table is not.

Low score or missing data?

Every piece of evidence carries a state, so the two can be told apart:

Confirmed a source shows it, and the link is in the dossier.

Assumed inferred from something else, such as size from retail reach.

Judgement calls

These are my calls rather than scraped data:

  • The weights for brand fit and the points for intro path. The call-first group survives every weighting tested; the middle of the table does not, and the Method tab reports how far each scenario moves it.
  • Which brands are over £100m revenue.
  • The 9-month half-life for news and the 1.5× stacking cap.
  • The group lines for Call first and Next.

Known gaps

Most small UK companies don't publish turnover, so size comes from the accounts type filed at Companies House, with retail reach as the stand-in. A board link needs the person's name on both the fund's team page and the brand's Companies House officer list, so a partner missing from the team page is missed. Job boards add little: these brands mostly hire through LinkedIn and recruiters.

    Procuros · UK go-to-market · Task 1.2 · six weeks from 2 November 2026

    Two funds, two ways in.

    We run the motion on the top two funds from the shortlist: Piper, a private equity firm, and Maven, a venture investor. The goal is the same for both: warm intros to grocery brands that need EDI. One thing differs on purpose, and that's how we ask.

    Why these two

    They're #1 and #2 on the shortlist, both have a fund person sitting on a target brand's board, and one is PE while the other is VC, which is the contrast the brief asks for.

    The one thing we change

    Piper only backs consumer brands and has a team for portfolio talent, so we offer something to the whole portfolio. Consumer is a small slice of Maven's 155 companies, so a portfolio offer would be noise there. Instead we make one specific ask through the board director.

    Both funds go through the same six steps. Only step 2 and step 4 differ between them.

    One page per brand, built from evidence we already collected for Task 1.1. It shows which grocers the brand sells through, what it trades with them and what to ask. It's useful to the brand even if they never talk to us, and that's why a fund will forward it.

    The brands, with the evidence

    What could go wrong

    Luke isn't close to operations

    A board director knows the founders but not always who runs orders. The blurb asks the founder to pick the person.

    A single point of failure

    Everything rests on one brand. If Tom Parker says no, we use the no as a reason to ask Carl about Tiba Tempeh, and say why the first one didn't fit.

    It may already have a provider

    With five grocers, Tom Parker may already use an EDI provider. That's still a useful call: switching is a sale too, and it tells us about the category.

    How we'll know it worked

    Every stage has a number to hit and a date to hit it by. The early stages tell us quickly whether a lane is alive. The last one tells us whether it made money.

    Speed

    Days from the first email to the first intro. This is where we expect the single ask to win.

    Effort

    Hours spent per intro, counting prep. A portfolio offer costs more to build, so it has to earn more intros to be worth it.

    Repeat

    Whether the fund offers a second intro without being asked. That's the best sign a fund will scale as a channel.

    Kill criteria

    When to stop a lane, decided now so we don't argue about it later.

    Silence

    No reply by day 14

    If no contact has replied after two touches each, stop that fund. Start #3 Active Partners in the same lane, through Spencer Skinner on Northern Monk's board.

    Stalled

    No intro 14 days after the meeting

    Stop asking. Write down the reason they gave, because it's the most useful data the test produces.

    Wrong brand fit

    The first two brand calls don't need us

    If their orders already flow automatically, our brand-fit assumption is wrong for this kind of brand. Fix the scoring before Task 1.3.

    Relationship

    Anyone asks us to stop

    If a fund or founder says the intro felt pushed, stop at once and apologise. The fund is worth more than one brand.

    The read-out on 11 December

    How we pick the winner

    The lane with more qualified brands wins. If it's a tie, the one with the faster first intro wins, and if that's tied too, the one that cost fewer hours per intro. The winning lane becomes the default motion in Task 1.3.

    What two funds can't prove

    This is one PE and one VC firm, with different people and brands. It points a direction but it isn't proof. That's why we log every touch: date, fund, contact, channel, lane, outcome and the reason given.

    If both lanes fail

    Then the route isn't the problem. The offer or the brand-fit assumption is, and we go back to the brand calls before scaling anything.
